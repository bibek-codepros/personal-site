# PHASE B — Visual Identity

> "People may forget layouts.
> They rarely forget how something made them feel."

---

# Objective

HOME should have a visual identity that is immediately recognizable.

It should not feel like:

- a portfolio template
- a startup landing page
- a SaaS product
- a documentation website

Instead...

HOME should feel like opening someone's beautifully kept journal.

Warm.

Quiet.

Thoughtful.

Timeless.

---

# Design Philosophy

Every visual decision should support the story.

Nothing should exist only because it looks beautiful.

Everything should help the visitor slow down.

---

# Emotional Keywords

HOME should feel:

Warm

Honest

Human

Calm

Minimal

Reflective

Curious

Hopeful

Timeless

If a design element creates noise instead of calm,

it probably doesn't belong.

---

# Inspiration

Instead of copying websites,

take inspiration from experiences.

Think of:

• Reading a favorite book in a quiet café

• Writing inside a leather notebook

• Watching rain through an airplane window

• Looking through an old family photo album

• Drinking coffee during a slow Sunday morning

That is the feeling HOME should recreate.

---

# Typography

Typography is the primary design element.

Readers will spend more time reading than clicking.

Therefore typography deserves the most attention.

Principles

✓ Excellent readability

✓ Comfortable line length

✓ Generous spacing

✓ Clear hierarchy

✓ No decorative fonts

Words should become invisible.

Readers should forget they are reading typography.

---

# Photography

Photos should never feel like social media.

Instead,

they should feel documentary.

Real.

Natural.

Unedited.

Use photography to preserve memories,

not to impress visitors.

Examples

✓ Airport

✓ Mountains

✓ Bali

✓ Coffee

✓ Childhood

✓ Office moments

Never use photos simply to fill empty space.

Every photo should answer:

"Why is this memory important?"

---

# Illustration Style

Illustrations should be soft.

Minimal.

Editorial.

They should support the story,

never replace it.

Possible illustration subjects

• Airplane window

• Old Nokia phone

• Notebook

• Coffee mug

• Mountains

• Timeline moments

• Small location sketches

Avoid overly detailed artwork.

Less is more.

---

# Icons

Icons should feel hand-picked,

not downloaded.

Use one icon family throughout the project.

Icons should communicate,

not decorate.

---

# Color Philosophy

Colors should create comfort,

not excitement.

Background

Warm

Soft

Paper-inspired

Primary

Deep charcoal

Secondary

Muted gray

Accent

Earth tones

Warm blue

Forest green

Soft orange

Avoid highly saturated colors.

---

# Motion

Animation should feel like breathing.

Never like showing off.

Motion should be:

Slow

Intentional

Meaningful

Examples

Page transitions

Gentle fade

Notebook pages

Soft slide

Images

Subtle reveal

Buttons

Small interaction

Motion should reward curiosity,

not demand attention.

---

# White Space

White space is part of the design.

Do not fill every area.

Silence makes stories stronger.

HOME should feel comfortable to read.

Not dense.

Not busy.

---

# Sound

HOME intentionally has no background music.

Silence is part of the experience.

Visitors should hear their own thoughts.

---

# Brand Personality

If HOME were a person...

It wouldn't speak loudly.

It wouldn't interrupt.

It wouldn't try to impress.

It would simply say:

"Come in.

Take your time.

There's no rush."

---

# Visual Consistency

Every page should belong to the same world.

Homepage

↓

Becoming

↓

Notebook

↓

Projects

↓

Timeline

↓

Conversations

Visitors should never feel like they've entered another website.

---

# Success Criteria

Visual Identity is complete when:

✓ Every page feels connected.

✓ Every illustration has purpose.

✓ Every photograph tells a story.

✓ Every animation feels natural.

✓ HOME becomes recognizable without showing its logo.

At that point,

HOME no longer has a design.

It has an identity.
