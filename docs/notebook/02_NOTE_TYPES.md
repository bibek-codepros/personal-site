# NOTE TYPES

Status: Production Ready

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

Not every thought deserves the same presentation.

Some thoughts are one sentence.

Some become reflections.

Some preserve memories.

Some ask questions.

This document defines every supported note type inside HOME.

Every future notebook entry should belong to one of these types.

---

# Philosophy

Notebook should feel varied.

Readers should never feel like they're reading the same format repeatedly.

Different thoughts deserve different spaces.

Different emotions deserve different layouts.

---

# Type 01

## One Line

The shortest format.

Usually one sentence.

Simple.

Memorable.

Examples:

> Life didn't change overnight.
> My perspective did.

---

> Being busy isn't the same as moving forward.

---

Presentation:

Large typography.

Lots of whitespace.

Centered.

Maximum:

25 words.

---

# Type 02

## Reflection

A short paragraph.

Usually one personal realization.

Examples:

After leading people for a while, I realised that good leadership isn't about having answers.

It's about making people feel safe enough to ask questions.

---

Length:

80–250 words.

Most common note type.

---

# Type 03

## Memory

A tiny story.

Usually tied to a place or object.

Examples:

The first Starbucks.

The old Nokia phone.

The boarding pass.

The first HTML file.

Unlike Becoming,

these memories don't tell the whole story.

They preserve a moment.

---

Length:

100–300 words.

---

# Type 04

## Lesson

A lesson discovered through experience.

Never advice.

Never motivation.

Simply:

"This is what I learned."

Example:

I spent three years studying networking.

My career became web development.

Life doesn't always reward perfect planning.

Sometimes it rewards staying open.

---

# Type 05

## Question

Notebook doesn't always answer.

Sometimes it asks.

Examples:

Why do we remember some failures more clearly than our successes?

---

When was the last time you changed your mind about something important?

Questions invite readers into the notebook.

---

# Type 06

## Observation

Small details.

Usually things people overlook.

Examples:

The cleanest airports also felt the quietest.

---

The best conversations usually happen after the coffee is finished.

---

Observations make Notebook feel alive.

---

# Type 07

## Travel Note

Travel isn't about destinations.

It's about perspective.

Examples:

My first ocean.

The heat in Singapore.

The taste of coffee in Bali.

The bargaining story.

Travel notes should capture feelings,

not itineraries.

---

# Type 08

## Work Note

Thoughts discovered while working.

Not productivity advice.

Not management frameworks.

Simply experience.

Example:

Sometimes solving the technical problem is easier than helping two people understand each other.

---

# Type 09

## Leadership Note

Leadership through experience.

Never through authority.

Examples:

People remember how meetings felt.

Not how long they lasted.

---

Leadership notes should remain humble.

---

# Type 10

## Random Thought

Some ideas don't fit anywhere.

Keep them.

Notebook should feel human.

Not categorized.

Example:

I still enjoy opening a brand-new notebook,

even if I don't know what I'll write inside.

---

# Metadata

Every note should support optional metadata.

Future format:

---

title:

category:

type:

date:

location:

readingTime:

tags:

featured:

---

Metadata should support future filtering,

search,

and recommendations.

---

# Category Balance

Notebook should remain balanced.

Avoid filling it with only:

Leadership

or

Work.

Life is wider than a career.

Aim for variety.

---

# Future Collections

As HOME grows,

Notebook may include collections.

Examples:

Lessons From Bali

Coffee Thoughts

Conversations I Remember

Mistakes Worth Keeping

Letters To Myself

Things I Changed My Mind About

Collections make Notebook feel like a living archive.

---

# Writing Rules

Always write naturally.

Never sound like a motivational speaker.

Never sound like LinkedIn.

Never sound like AI.

If a sentence doesn't sound like Bibek saying it over coffee,

rewrite it.

---

# Reader Experience

Notebook should create this feeling:

"I'll just read one."

↓

"Oh...

this one's interesting."

↓

"Maybe one more."

↓

Twenty minutes later...

they're still reading.

That effortless curiosity is the goal.

---

# Definition of Done

The Note System succeeds when readers stop noticing the format—

and simply begin collecting thoughts that stay with them long after they close the page.
