# Method

This map does not ask "have we said this before." It asks the question `NARRATIVE_OWNERSHIP.md` set up for it: **for every recurring life event, is each section doing its own job, or is one section quietly doing another section's job?**

Built from a complete read of the Homepage, all six Becoming chapters, and all twenty Notebook entries. Sixteen recurring life events were found. No content has been changed. Nothing has been shortened, moved, or removed — this document only recommends.

**A finding, stated plainly before the detail:** most of the site's apparent repetition is not actually a violation. Of the sixteen events mapped, eleven show the sections doing genuinely different jobs — Becoming connecting, Notebook isolating, Homepage inviting — the way `NARRATIVE_OWNERSHIP.md` intends. Five show a real ownership violation, where one section is quietly retelling another section's version of the same moment at the same scale. Those five deserve the real attention in Milestone 2 onward. The other eleven should very likely stay exactly where they are.

---

# How To Read The Verdicts

- **Correctly Owned** — one section holds this material, appropriately, and no other section duplicates it.
- **Legitimate Multi-Angle** — the same event appears more than once, but each appearance does a different job (a different scale, a different facet, a different moment in time). This is `NARRATIVE_OWNERSHIP.md` working as intended. Not a problem.
- **Ownership Violation** — the same event, at the same scale, doing the same job, in two sections. This is the actual definition of repetition under this framework, and the only category that warrants a real editorial decision.
- **Gap** — a section is missing material it should probably own.

# The Flagship Cases

## 1. Bali — sensory detail vs. Becoming ch.5 (Highest priority)

**Where it appears:** Homepage (Quote Moment, Window Seat sections — compressed) → Becoming ch.5 "Window Seat" (404 lines, contains every sub-scene below) → Notebook #12 "My First Window Seat," #13 "The First Ocean," #14 "Eight Million Rupiah," #15 "Bali Taught Me To Slow Down," #18 "My Mum Was Right."

**Ownership analysis, entry by entry:**
- **#12** retells ch.5's boarding-rain-selfie-window sequence in the same order, at the same scale, with no new facet. **Violation.**
- **#13** gives the ocean a dedicated, quieter treatment ch.5 only mentions in passing. This is Notebook noticing something Becoming moved past. **Correctly Owned — leave untouched.**
- **#14** retells ch.5's currency/slippers anecdote with the same numbers, same beats. **Violation.**
- **#15** retells ch.5's Starbucks/milk-tea/Hindu-culture material closely, though its closing angle (bringing home "a different pace of mind" rather than souvenirs) is slightly more its own. **Violation, but the mildest of the three.**
- **#18** takes the mum's-quote scene and pivots to a genuinely later, wiser reading of it ("she wasn't just talking about Bali. She was talking about life"). This is Notebook doing its actual job — a small memory, reflected on, at a different moment than Becoming reflects on it. **Correctly Owned — leave untouched.**

**Why this cluster is the largest single decision in Version 2:** ch.5 is already the chapter the audit named as needing more gravity — and it currently spends its length on the very sensory details (ocean, currency, Starbucks) that Notebook has *also* claimed. Becoming ch.5 doing Notebook's granular-detail job is very likely the reason it currently reads as "thin despite being long" — its length is spent on breadth it doesn't need to own, rather than depth on the one moment (the window, the realization) that's actually irreplaceable.

**Recommendation:** Should move. Specifically — trim ch.5's ownership of the *specific* sensory beats (exact currency figures, the Starbucks/milk-tea detail) that Notebook #12/14/15 already carry, and let those Notebook entries stand as the record of record for that detail. This does two things at once: gives ch.5 more room for the window-seat realization itself (the audit's actual ask), and resolves three ownership violations without deleting anything Notebook readers currently value. #13 and #18 need no change at all. This is not a small edit — flag for your direct input before Milestone 2 begins, per the plan's existing note.

**Should it remain repeated?** Partially, and on purpose — #13 and #18 should stay exactly as they are, because they already prove the ownership model works when a Notebook entry finds its own angle. #12, #14, #15 should not remain repeated at their current scale.

## 2. Three Army Interviews (High priority)

**Where it appears:** Homepage One Minute Story (two lines, correctly compressed) → Becoming ch.1 "Where It All Began" (the full connective version — this failure is *why* everything after it exists) → Notebook #4 "Three Army Interviews" (physical exam, IQ test, written exam, interview, three full attempts — essentially the same structure as ch.1, only slightly shorter).

**Ownership analysis:** Homepage is doing its job correctly — nothing to change. Becoming ch.1 is doing its job correctly — this is the right home for the complete causal version. Notebook #4 is the violation: it isn't finding a smaller, different facet of this memory, it's retelling the same three-attempt structure Becoming already owns, just compressed.

**Why not simply cut it:** this is the single most important event in the entire story, and a reader who only ever browses Notebook — never clicking into Becoming — would otherwise never encounter it at all. Deleting it trades one problem (repetition) for a worse one (a Notebook reader missing the site's most load-bearing memory entirely).

**Recommendation:** Should remain, but should become smaller and more specific rather than shorter-and-the-same. Notebook's version should zoom into one facet ch.1 doesn't have room to sit with — the three days of silence afterward, a single physical detail, one specific moment of that silence — rather than re-walking all three attempts. This is a reangle, not a trim and not a deletion.

**Risk note:** this is emotionally load-bearing material. Any change here should be tested against the ending-technique and co-author principles in `EDITORIAL_LANGUAGE.md` before it's considered finished, not just against this ownership question.

## 3. "The Place That Believed In Me" — same title, three times (High priority)

**Where it appears:** Homepage Code Pros section → Becoming ch.3 (same title, full chapter) → Notebook #8 (same title again).

**Ownership analysis:** Homepage and Becoming ch.3 are both doing their jobs correctly, and both have been independently flagged, by every review pass so far, as HOME's strongest material. Protect both without question. Notebook #8 is the actual finding here: its closing thesis — "Some companies build products. Some quietly build people. I'm grateful mine did both." — is essentially ch.3's own thesis ("Companies create employees. The right environment helps create people.") in slightly different words, reached via a shorter version of the same beats.

**Why this one needs unusually careful handling:** this isn't a minor scene overlapping — it's the site's single most important insight, appearing a third time under the identical title. The risk isn't to Notebook #8 itself. The risk is to ch.3 and the Homepage section: an idea this central loses some of its weight the third time it's stated as a thesis rather than lived through as a scene.

**Recommendation:** Two honest options, not one prescription — this is a bigger call than a trim, and I'd rather name the fork than pick unilaterally:
- (a) Re-angle Notebook #8 toward one specific, small memory from those early days — an actual task, an actual mistake, an actual afternoon — rather than restating the "environment vs. company" thesis a third time.
- (b) Accept that Becoming ch.3 and the Homepage already own this completely, and let Notebook simply not have its own entry for this particular event.

Both are legitimate. (a) preserves Notebook's completeness across the timeline; (b) is more honest about the fact that not every major memory needs to exist at all three scales. Flagging for your decision before Milestone 2.

## 4. Copying HTML (High priority)

**Where it appears:** Becoming ch.2 "Starting Again" (middle section, part of the connective arc toward the boilerplate and, eventually, leadership) → Notebook #6 "Copying HTML" (same scene — copied a teammate's file, deleted things, confused by `<div>` elements — closing on a very similar insight about beginnings).

**Ownership analysis:** Becoming ch.2 is correctly using this scene as one link in a longer chain. Notebook #6 doesn't add a new facet — it retells the same anecdote at nearly the same scale, arriving at nearly the same conclusion.

**Why this is worth fixing rather than leaving:** this is exactly the material the 25-year-old-developer reader (from the earlier creative review) responded to most. It's high-value, not fragile — a good candidate for actually improving rather than just trimming.

**Recommendation:** Should move toward specificity. What's the one detail only a diary would bother noticing — a single tag, a single bug, the exact moment of embarrassment — that the Becoming version, busy building toward its bigger payoff, doesn't have room for? Should not be deleted; the developer-reader value is real.

**Why HOME exists — the closing thesis (Becoming ch.6 ending; Notebook #20 "Home").** Each is the appropriate closing statement for its own container — a reader who only finishes Becoming needs its own sense of arrival, and so does a reader who only finishes Notebook Volume 1. The one soft note: the actual phrasing ("I wanted to build something different" / "I wanted a place that felt honest") echoes closely enough that reading both back to back is noticeable. Low priority — a polish-level word choice pass at most, in Milestone 7, not a structural change.

# A Gap, Not A Repetition

Three of the six Becoming chapters — "Starting Again," "Leading Beyond Code," and "Still Becoming" — have no dedicated Homepage section. They're reachable only through the chapter-card menu. Meanwhile "The Place That Believed In Me" and "Window Seat" each get a full, dedicated Homepage section of their own.

This isn't a repetition problem — it's the opposite: a place where Homepage may be *under*-owning its job of emotional invitation. Whether this is intentional curation (feature the two strongest chapters, let the rest be discovered) or an oversight is worth a direct decision rather than an assumption. Flagging here rather than guessing.
