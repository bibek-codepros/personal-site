# The Place That Believed In Me

There are moments in life that quietly divide everything into two parts.

The person you were before.

And the person you slowly became afterward.

For me, one of those moments arrived when I walked into Code Pros.

At the time, I didn't realize it.

I thought I had simply joined a company.

Looking back now, I understand that I had found something much more valuable.

I had found a place that was willing to grow alongside me.

---

When I first joined, I wasn't the person people know today.

I wasn't confident.

I wasn't experienced.

I wasn't ready for bigger responsibilities.

I was simply someone trying to become a little better than yesterday.

Every day, I learned something new.

Sometimes it was technical.

Sometimes it had nothing to do with code.

I slowly discovered that building websites was only a small part of the journey.

Learning how to think.

How to communicate.

How to solve problems.

How to take ownership.

Those lessons would stay with me far longer than any programming language.

---

One thing I will always be grateful for is this.

People saw potential in me long before I could clearly see it in myself.

That changes a person.

When someone chooses to trust you with responsibility, they also give you something invisible.

Confidence.

Not the loud kind.

The quiet kind.

The kind that slowly grows every time someone says,

"I think you can handle this."

And somehow...

you do.

---

Responsibility never arrived all at once.

It came one small task at a time.

One project.

One deadline.

One difficult conversation.

One unexpected challenge.

Looking back, none of those moments felt extraordinary.

But together, they quietly transformed the way I saw myself.

I stopped asking,

"Can I do this?"

and slowly started asking,

"How can I do this better?"

That small change in thinking changed everything.

---

As the years passed, my role naturally evolved.

I began as someone focused on frontend development.

Then I found myself solving operational problems.

Helping people.

Improving workflows.

Supporting projects beyond code.

None of this had been part of the plan I imagined years earlier.

Life simply kept introducing new opportunities.

I kept saying yes.

Every new responsibility felt slightly larger than the last.

That was probably the point.

Growth rarely arrives after we feel ready.

It usually arrives first.

Confidence catches up later.

---

Whenever people ask me about becoming a COO, I often feel they are asking the wrong question.

Titles are easy to describe.

Transformation is much harder.

The title is simply where the journey is today.

It is not the journey itself.

What matters far more to me is everything that happened before it.

Every mistake.

Every late night.

Every difficult decision.

Every conversation that taught me how much I still had to learn.

Those are the things that shaped me.

Not the title on a business card.

---

If I had to describe Code Pros in one sentence, it would not be,

"The company where I work."

It would be,

"The place that gave me room to become someone I didn't know I could become."

There is a very important difference.

Companies create employees.

The right environment helps create people.

I was fortunate enough to experience the second.

---

One lesson has stayed with me ever since.

Talent matters.

Hard work matters.

Discipline matters.

But sometimes, the greatest turning point in someone's life is simply meeting people who believe in them before they fully believe in themselves.

I hope every person experiences that at least once.

Because it has the power to change everything.

It certainly changed me.

---

Sometimes people ask what the biggest opportunity in my career has been.

They expect me to mention a project.

Or a promotion.

Or a title.

The truth is much simpler.

The biggest opportunity I ever received was the chance to grow.

Not into a better developer.

Not into a better manager.

Into a better version of myself.

For that, I will always be grateful.

---

When I look back at the person who copied his first HTML file because he didn't know where to begin...

and then look at the responsibilities I carry today...

I don't see two different people.

I see thousands of ordinary days connected together.

Small improvements.

Small lessons.

Small acts of trust.

Over time, those small moments quietly became a life.

And without realizing it...

I had already started building a home I never planned for.

The story, however, was still far from over.
